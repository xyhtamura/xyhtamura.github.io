# *Titanopsis calcarea bizarre* — work notes

- **Year:** 2018
- **Form:** head-to-toe wearable suite; three garments, accessories, cosmetics,
  hair, nails, and shoes
- **Occasion:** a friend's succulent-themed wedding
- **Collaborator:** George Palmos, garment design and construction; exact credit
  division to confirm
- **Documentation:** `../tcb.jpg` and `../img/TitanopsisRing.jpg`
- **Source status:** reconstructed from Xyh Tamura's oral account on 2026-08-14.
  Product names, botanical nomenclature, dates, and the division of construction
  work should be checked against surviving records before detailed publication.

## Working account

*Titanopsis calcarea bizarre* was developed over several months for a friend's
succulent-themed wedding. The couple supplied a palette of sandy and earthy
colors. Xyh treated the occasion as a reason to construct a complete look rather
than assemble an outfit from existing clothes. Friends associated Xyh with a
deliberately conspicuous, Lady Gaga-like event presence, and making a substantial
look was understood as a gift to the couple and to the occasion.

The project began with cosmetics. A beige nail polish with a green shimmer,
visible in the ring photograph, supplied the first color relation. Contemporary
images of greyed or corpse-like lip colors, possibly an OCC Lip Tar product,
added a desaturated beige-grey register. A photograph identified at the time as
*Titanopsis calcarea* “bizarre” then organized those cosmetic choices into a
plant-based palette. The plant resembled both a living stone and coral: bluish
grey and brick-red bodies appeared to produce neon green, tentacle-like growths.

The resulting palette had four main colors:

| Color | Role in the suite |
|---|---|
| Acid or neon green | Highest-saturation accent; trousers, coat appliqué, eye makeup, and highlights |
| Metallic steel blue | Inner garment, coat appliqué, lace closure, and a cyan-shifting reflective surface |
| Brick or stone red | Small coat appliqués recalling the plant's stone-like bodies |
| Greyed beige | Coat ground, shoes, lip and skin register; a neutral closer to porcelain, earth, or squid skin than an ordinary skin-tone beige |

The contrast between the greyed neutrals and the acid green was the main visual
axis. Iridescent and reflective surfaces formed a secondary system: green
shimmer over beige nails, metallic blue cloth, a brown-to-yellow-green shot
lining, and the multichrome cockroach ring all changed color with angle and
light.

## Garments and construction

The suite consists of a coat, inner garment, and trousers.

### Coat

Xyh specified a fitted waist and volume around the hips to produce a more
androgynous silhouette. The coat uses a carefully selected greyed beige ground.
Neon green forms emerge from its sides and corners, while metallic blue and
brick-red appliqués repeat the plant palette. Xyh developed the arrangement in a
Photoshop sketch.

The coat was designed without a regular straight hem. Its wavy front opening
exposes the metallic blue inner garment, which Xyh described as reading like a
river or tributary through the beige field. The front does not close completely.
A steel-blue lace holds the two sides together, with a clasp at the upper chest
for added security.

Xyh's first sketch had sharper shoulders. Palmos proposed a rounded shoulder
because sharply structured shoulders were already common in 2018. The revision
gave the coat a looser, partly streetwear silhouette. Palmos also helped resolve
the lace-and-clasp closure and the garment's construction.

The appliqués produced a flatter surface than Xyh intended. Because their
materials and relief were too similar to the base, they sometimes read as a
printed pattern. A future reconstruction should make appliqué materially
necessary through padding, relief, edge treatment, pile, translucent layers, or
stronger differences in fabric texture. The metallic blue areas were initially
imagined as washed denim, which would also have increased the material contrast,
but suitable denim was not acquired.

### Inner garment

The inner garment uses reflective steel-blue fabric with a cyan or foil-like
shift. The material choice drew partly on Y2K styling and the blue metallic
surface of Madonna's *Ray of Light* cover. Its visibility through the coat's
irregular opening makes the inner layer part of the exterior composition.

### Trousers

The trousers use the same acid-green fabric as the green coat appliqués. Palmos
cut or stitched them with excess length so the narrow legs form asymmetrical
folds rather than a single straight line. They were conceived as wearable
separates, although Xyh avoided wearing or washing them because the intense green
pigment was expected to dull.

### Lining and remaining materials

Xyh and Palmos found an iridescent shot-effect fabric shifting between brown and
pale yellow-green. It did not fit any exterior role already assigned in the
design, so Palmos used it as the coat lining. This kept the material in the suite
and gave movement at the coat edge another color shift. Whether the fabric is
true shot silk or a synthetic shot fabric remains to be confirmed.

The shoes were sourced secondhand. Their cream or milky khaki color continues
the coat's neutral ground. They were a practical substitute rather than Xyh's
first choice, although they read coherently in the surviving photograph.

## Accessories, cosmetics, and hair

The iridescent cockroach ring is made from a secondhand plastic toy coated in
multichrome nail polish and attached to a metal ring base. The polish makes the
toy resemble an iridescent beetle while linking it directly to the nails that
supplied the suite's first color relation.

Xyh also modified a kitchen glove by removing the fingertips and powdering or
aging its surface. The glove became close enough to Xyh's skin color that it is
easy to miss in photographs. It added another artificial skin layer without
introducing a new color.

The makeup repeated the garment palette: neon green eyelids and highlighter,
muted surrounding tones, and a greyed beige “dead” lip. The nails used the beige
polish with green shimmer that initiated the project. Xyh bleached their hair
for the first time and had it colored grey, extending the desaturated palette to
the head. The styling therefore did not finish an already complete garment; it
continued the same palette and material logic across the whole body.

## Reference field

These references belong to Xyh's recollection of the design process. Exact
products and dates remain to be verified.

- A photographed *Titanopsis calcarea* described as a “bizarre” variant, with
  neon green growths and blue-grey, beige, and brick-red stone-like bodies.
- Beige nail polish with a green shimmer; brand and shade unknown.
- The expanding availability of chrome and multichrome nail polish in the 2010s;
  ILNP was part of Xyh's awareness of the finish.
- A greyed, wet or corpse-like OCC Cosmetics Lip Tar image; exact shade and the
  finish finally worn are uncertain.
- Madonna's *Ray of Light* cover as a cue for metallic blue fabric.
- Y2K metallics, Atomic Age imagery, desert palettes, and *Fallout: New Vegas*.
- Coral, squid skin, living stones, tentacles, rivers, and tributaries as visual
  rather than narrative references.

## Authorship and collaboration

Xyh originated the decision to make a total look, selected the plant reference,
developed the palette and silhouette, produced the Photoshop sketch, chose
materials with Palmos, and made the styling, cosmetics, hair, nail, glove, shoe,
and ring decisions. Palmos collaborated on garment design and construction. His
identified design contributions include the rounded shoulders, lace-and-clasp
closure, asymmetrical trouser folds, and use of the shot-effect fabric as lining.

Before public credit becomes more detailed than “with George Palmos,” confirm
who patterned, cut, sewed, and applied each element, and whether Palmos used a
studio or label name at the time.

## What the work establishes

1. **Cosmetics generated the garments.** Nail and lip surfaces were early design
   inputs, not finishing details added after the clothes.
2. **A photograph became a cross-media color system.** The plant image moved
   through nail polish, cloth, appliqué, lining, makeup, hair, a glove, shoes, and
   jewelry. Each material reproduced the palette differently.
3. **The work is a suite because the components share material rules.** Repeated
   fabrics and colors bind three garments and several accessories without making
   them identical.
4. **The occasion was part of the work.** The look answered a friend's wedding
   theme and an existing social expectation that Xyh would arrive through an
   intentionally constructed presence. Its extravagance operated as an
   occasion-specific gift.
5. **Irregularity was already an explicit design decision.** The wavy hem,
   incomplete opening, layered blue channel, and asymmetrical trouser folds avoid
   the regular edges of a conventional suit. Xyh recalls this as an infant stage
   of the later quantization counter-poetics. The 2018 garment is evidence of an
   emerging preference, not proof that the later theory was already complete.

## Retrospective assessment

The project succeeded most clearly as a total palette distributed across the
body. The acid green holds together details that vary substantially in material,
while the greyed beige, steel blue, brick red, and iridescent shifts keep the
green from becoming the only register. The rounded coat, narrow folded trousers,
metallic inner layer, hair, makeup, nails, glove, and ring make the surviving
photograph read as one designed event rather than a garment with accessories
added afterward.

The main unresolved problem is the appliqué. Its flatness weakens the reason for
using attached fabric instead of print. A new version should preserve the palette
and silhouette while giving the coat more relief, texture, or structural depth.
The existing work can therefore be documented as both a completed suite and a
prototype for a more materially differentiated reconstruction.

  status of the term is checked.

  when the finish entered the broader market, without product-history sources.

- Keep the quantization-counterpoetics connection dated as a retrospective
  account of an emerging 2018 concern.
