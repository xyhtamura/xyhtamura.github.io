Physa · Biomemristor as Instrument 2026

A browser instrument and extended abstract (September 2026) on the contested circuit model it implements

Physa is a monophonic browser instrument and input processor. A simulated charge-controlled memristor processes the audio path in parallel with an RC circuit modeling capacitive effects. When senescence is enabled, spectral energy feeds a shared wear map that deforms the memristive resistance curve over time, allowing incoming audio signals to alter the instrument's subsequent transfer function.

Biomemristor as Instrument traces a contested circuit model across materials science, biophysics, and computer music. In 2015 the slime mold Physarum polycephalum was reported as a biological memristor, initiating a decade of experimental sound works based on the claim. A 2025 characterization of 310 observations across 38 cultures demonstrated an absence of pinched hysteresis loops, attributing the elliptical curves to linear capacitance. The extended abstract specifies Physa directly from that dispute, contrasting the memristive model and the capacitive RC model as parallel sounding circuits for comparative performance.
