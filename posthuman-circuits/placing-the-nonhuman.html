Placing the Nonhuman

Green Colonialism, Ecoprimitivism, and Cosmotechnics

Xyh Tamura · Accepted for The Wound of the Possible, Noema Media

Contemporary ecological art often centers sensing, attention, and care, as if ecological entanglement were something viewers still need to discover. Green colonialism distributes that perspective unequally. Global South and non-western communities are required to adopt technologies authorized by Northern ecological transition while being framed as premodern custodians remaining in ecological authenticity. For communities already situated within technological, ecological, and colonial pressures, the question is less how to notice relation than what can be built, adapted, or appropriated from within it.

This division between nature and technics also shapes which aesthetic philosophies circulate. Chinese ecoaesthetics, written in English for Anglophone ecocriticism, sees environmental experience through deep ecology and Russian Formalism while excising technology entirely. Yet technics already structures ecological and cosmological relations. Yuk Hui’s cosmotechnics names this plurality: different traditions organize technical activity, cosmos, and moral life differently. What counts as nature, technology, or authentic indigeneity is an aesthetic classification applied at the point of reception.

Across the Global South, technical objects are already integrated into cosmological and political life. During Vishwakarma pujas in Indian industrial workplaces, mechanics consecrate machinery as active participants: at a Guwahati refinery, sixteen altars correspond to sixteen processing units, and technicians call the deity “the god of infrastructure.” In Malay free-trade-zone factories, assembly-line spirit possession and microscopes that “disclosed spirits lurking within” articulate the disciplinary strain of machine labor. In the Ecuadorian Amazon, Ursula Biemann and Paulo Tavares’s Forest Law demonstrates that forest personhood is established through technical and legal processes rather than pastoral innocence.

Ecotechnoaesthetics names a shift from ecological revelation to situated operation. Technical systems need not estrange people and humans from nature. They can also provide the material ground from which collective life is negotiated and rebuilt.
