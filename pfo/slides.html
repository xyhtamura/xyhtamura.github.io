Xyh Tamura

Artist-researcher · singer-songwriter · creative technologist

Xyh Tamura is a Filipino/Ilonggo-Japanese composer, singer-songwriter, and artist-researcher based in Manila. The practice investigates voice, unquantized pitch notation, and physical acoustics across experimental pop, custom vocal instruments, browser synthesis engines, and musical theater. Rather than working inside supplied commercial formats, Xyh authors the apparatus: the acoustic instrument, notation language in hertz, recursive meter grammar, or digital waveguide model.

Desiderata — vocal ambient album · written, performed, produced, mixed, and mastered by Xyh Tamura · selected for The Wrong Eclipse, curated by David Quiles Guilló
xyhnthesizer — Kontakt vocal organ sampled from Xyh's singing
The Unbounded Organ — digital waveguide instrument evaluated across pipe lengths from 8.575 mm to 40,075 km

Desiderata, 2026.

Desiderata uses only acoustic voice and breath as source material. Vocalizations are time-stretched, granulated, layered, and microtonally vocoded against ocean and insect field recordings. The companion xyhnthesizer vocal organ samples sustained phonemes and breath registers directly from the tracking sessions. Building the instrument and composing the record ran concurrently: tracking revealed which sample splits and formant filters were needed, while the organ supplied the polyphonic substrate for the arrangements.

The Unbounded Organ evaluates digital waveguide equations across pipe lengths from 8.575 mm to 40,075 km. The same acoustic model moves from musical pitch through diffuse spatial echo to planetary infrasonic drift. Synthesis code is treated as an aesthetic commitment and formal acoustic theory, linking physics and instrument building.

Music practice

Experimental Pop and Performance 2009–2026

The solo music practice integrates songwriting, vocal performance, keyboard synthesis, algorithmic score generation, and visual design into a single production workflow.

Pacing To (2015), produced with Brazilian artist MASM, is a six-track synthpop release developed through remote file exchange and mixed through cassette saturation and bandwidth limiting. Other work spans total serialism (Hyacinth), meterless vocal loops (Lie), granular synthesis (Know Me Do), vaporwave (Piyesta Plaza 1985 to 1995: Airwaves), and the Asian Composers League commission Meiosis (2009).

Music, performance, and tools

Eosforos 2025–2026

Three-track release as xyhtochrome · Saronay, Kulintang, and Tambi by Tusa Montes · live audiovisual performance for Diana Aviado's Kaos Magick · “Theia” selected for The Wrong Eclipse

Eosforos models the unstable physics of the Hadean eon across sound, custom software, and live performance. On the album, “Init” processes Y2K operating-system chimes into low-frequency drones; “Roil” pairs chaotic synthesis with sampled Saronay, Kulintang, and Tambi recorded by Tusa Montes; and “Theia” models protoplanetary collision through microtonal singing, noise, and synthesized percussion. Live performances for Diana Aviado's Kaos Magick linked this audio directly to custom browser tools: the aperiodic oscillator Roil, the scale-free melody generator Antemelos, and the audio-reactive WebGL visualizer Hadean Flare.

Formats and spatial notation

Tabota · Cycla · Stanzuary 2025→

Open score format and notation language · recursive metrical grammar · spatial score and text editor

Tabota is a plain-text score format and JavaScript runtime that stores pitch continuously in hertz. It encodes unquantized vocal glides, microtonal inflections, Bézier pitch contours, spatial trajectories, and media triggers as temporal objects.

Cycla is a formal grammar and parser for nested, additive, and non-isochronous time structures. Compact .cyc expressions define rhythm trees and fractional phase shifts that compile into Tabota timelines.

Stanzuary extends Tabota from arranging musical events in time to arranging items in three-dimensional space.

Browser instruments · physical simulation

Cytophones 2026

Family of seven browser instruments in which 2D physics simulations drive Web Audio synthesis

A family of seven browser instruments in which 2D physics simulations drive Web Audio synthesis. Fluid boundaries, cellular membranes, flocking particles, and botanical pores become playable sounding bodies whose collisions, deformations, and positions control resonators, delay networks, and waveguides.

Philippine timbres and environmental data feeds inform the synthesis architecture: ocean swell models, tidal ranges, and seismic telemetry perturb the physical membranes, turning continuous simulation into immediate, gestural acoustic performance.

Song and presentation

Night Bus · intermedia musicopoetics 2025

Song within Of Another Shore · presented at Saliksik-Musika II, University of the Philippines, June 2025

Night Bus is an electroacoustic song and performance paper from the intermedia suite Of Another Shore. The composition maps Tagalog vocal gestures across urban nocturnal transit, combining enjambed lyric phrasing, asemic vocality, microtonal synth ostinatos, and granular engine noise. Presented at Saliksik-Musika II at UP Diliman, the accompanying research analyzes how metrical patterns from historical Tagalog rowing songs (soliranin) and street chants (indolanin) operate as rhythmic memory in contemporary commuter spaces where archival audio recordings do not exist.

Film and live score

Manifest 2024–2025

Short film and videopoem by Bea Mariano and Xyh Tamura · live score by Xyh Tamura · presented at Search Mindscape Foundation and WHYNoT

A collaborative short film and videopoem by Bea Mariano and Xyh Tamura, Manifest montages the underwater wreck of the USS Samuel B. Roberts, high-rise construction across Metro Manila, microbial cultures, and archival military footage. The title activates four simultaneous registers: the cargo manifest, ghostly manifestation, materialization, and American manifest destiny in the Pacific. Performed live with analog synthesizers, voice, and hydrophone samples, the score layers asynchronous drone textures and polyrhythmic pulses that track visual cuts while sustaining autonomous musical development.

Screened to an audience, and at WHYNoT with the printed programme.

Musical theater

The Magic Staff 1999

Meralco Theatre · conceived and written at age nine · librettist, songwriter, and lead actor · with Fides Cuyugan-Asensio, Bernardo Bernardo, Monique Wilson, and other Philippine theater artists

Staged at the Meralco Theatre in Metro Manila in 1999, The Magic Staff is a three-act musical whose libretto and songs Xyh wrote at age nine, performing the lead role. Its development into a professional production joined libretto writing, songwriting, vocal performance, and staging in one process.

Produced by Okasaki Theaterworks · Victor Henry C. Tejero, director · Jose Gentica V, composer and musical director · Nonoy Froilan, choreographer · Salvador Bernal, sets and costumes · Naomi Shoko Matsumoto, lighting · PJ Aranador, art direction

Equation-first synthesis

Anexacta 2026→

Anexacta is a collection of browser instruments that expose mathematical and physical models as playable sound engines.

Aliquoto

Computes additive partial distributions across just-intonation ratios and geometric series.

Cella

Simulates two-dimensional wave propagation across coupled cellular grids to generate acoustic reverberation.

Moire

Maps visual spatial frequencies and interference patterns to microtonal sidebands.

Physa

Models memristive state transitions whose nonlinear resistance introduces hysteresis into the audio path.
