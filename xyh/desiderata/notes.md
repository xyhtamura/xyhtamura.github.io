# Desiderata

Working notes for an album by **XYH**.

> This is a holding document for process, poetics, source material, and ideas
> that may later be revised or moved elsewhere. It is not final album copy.

## Current shape

- Five tracks, all made. The album is finished and uploaded to Bandcamp.
- Total runtime is roughly 25–30 minutes, probably just under 30. Not yet
  confirmed against the uploaded files.
- Planned destination: submission to *The Wrong* (Eclipse).
- The central idea is a sequence of vocal fleshscapes: drone-like, evolving
  ambient landscapes made from voice and breath.
- The voice may be stretched, layered, pitch-shifted, reverberated, reversed,
  or otherwise manipulated, but it remains the primary material.
- The album should stay close to drone. If a piece draws too much attention to
  melody, harmonic progression, or conventional song form, it may not belong.

The "only voice and breath" rule is not literally true — track 5 brings in
stone, ocean, wind, metal, EMF — but the rule it breaks is not the one it
appears to break. Nothing on the album is heard as itself. There is no straight
field recording anywhere: the ocean in track 5 is an ocean with a voice through
it. So the accurate statement is not that everything is voice, but that nothing
sounds without voice fused into it. See **Porosity, not purity** below.

## Track list as poem

The track list is also a five-line poem. Each line is a track title:

> I could begin wanting  
> anything about this  
> world wet stone  
> undigested through one  
> mouth out the other

The line lengths have to work simultaneously as poetry and as song titles.
Their cuts and enjambment are active parts of the poem.

## Initial image: iridescent flesh

The album began with a visual rather than a sonic inspiration: iridescent
fleshscapes.

Iridescence feels organic and body-related, but also slightly unnatural. It
frames the body by accentuating qualities that are present but not always
associated with flesh. Skin can be unexpectedly colorful under particular
light.

Part of this image comes from thinking about my own olive skin tone. It tends
to look more desaturated, while foundation can lean too pink or too orange;
lipstick can produce a similar mismatch. From this came the image of
desaturated flesh becoming iridescent under light.

The sonic rhyme for that image is something totally vocal but manipulated into
a droning landscape: pure vocal material made eerie, textural, spectral, and
slowly evolving.

## Voice as ambient material

The reference field includes Björk’s *Medúlla* and Meredith Monk, but the aim
here is distinct: to use voice as the primary source of a drone and to make
vocal work function within an ambient listening context.

Voice is usually performative and foregrounded. Here it becomes sonic material
or something closer to an environment—an evolving, relatively blank landscape
that can behave like wallpaper without becoming completely still.

This also means working within an ambient sensibility: thinking about how the
music is used and heard, and paying attention to texture and spectral range
rather than treating melody, rhythm, and harmonic progression as the main
points of orientation. A slowly stretched melody may stop registering as a
melody at all. Changes can occur over ten or twenty seconds, leaving tones that
layer and fill the bass, middle, and upper ranges.

Many ambient musicians approach the form through electronics, field recording,
or sound practice rather than vocal performance. *Desiderata* brings a
vocalist’s material into that culture of listening.

### Where it lands

The origin references and the landing references are not the same, and only the
second kind is useful to a listener deciding whether to press play.

**Nearest finished record:** Tim Hecker, *Love Streams* (2016). Choral source
recorded from real singers and processed into a spectral mass that is not a song
and is deliberately uncanny — the same operation as this album, from the
opposite direction. He starts with a choir and removes the human; I start with
one voice and multiply it into a field.

**Same room:**

- **Sarah Davachi** — slow, austere, tuning-led, melancholy without consolation.
  The closest match for what tracks 2 and 4 are doing with intonation.
- **Kara-Lis Coverdale**, *Grafts* — voice, organ and digital processing,
  sacred-adjacent without being devotional.
- **Félicia Atkinson** — voice-forward, intimate, bodily, non-song.
- **Lustmord** — the dark ambient anchor, and a cavernous processed-choir sound.
- **Grouper** — the darker version of the Barwick method: voice and reverb,
  unresolved rather than luminous.

**Julianna Barwick** is the right technique and the wrong affect. Loop the voice
into a cathedral is the nearest method to hand, and hers is hymnal, consonant,
and builds toward something. This album is none of those. "Barwick without the
hope, and without the songs" is a usable one-line positioning precisely because
the technique is close enough for the difference to be the information.

**Lineage, for voice as material rather than performance:** Joan La Barbara,
Maja S. K. Ratkje's *Voice*, Pamela Z, Charlemagne Palestine's sung drones, and
Pauline Oliveros's *Deep Listening*, recorded in a cistern — a voice and a
resonant space becoming inseparable, which is what track 3 is.

**Mechanism-specific, per track:**

- **Alvin Lucier, *I Am Sitting in a Room*** — the conceptual anchor for **The
  self becomes a world**, and for the bathroom recording. A voice is replaced,
  by degrees, with the resonance of the space it is in.
- **William Basinski, *The Disintegration Loops*** — for *undigested through
  one*. Material irreversibly ageing during the transfer is exactly the rot in
  the Gurgulator, arrived at by tape rather than by code.
- **Éliane Radigue** and **Phill Niblock** — for *anything about this*. No
  events, only a later point at which it has changed; and beating between close
  partials as the actual content.
- **Kali Malone** and **Ellen Arkbro** — just intonation drone, for track 4's
  JI↔12-TET coherence swing.

### Two moments, extended

The reference field is more precise than "Medúlla and Meredith Monk." It is two
particular moments, and both are moments that pass.

The opening of Björk's "An Echo, A Stain" (*Vespertine*) is a choir on a minor
chord that then builds into the song. The end of "Where Is the Line"
(*Medúlla*) is a violent crescendo whose reverbed choir trails off across the
last few seconds. In both cases the question is the same one: what if that part
did not pass? What if the introduction is the whole song, or the tail goes on
long enough to become the piece?

That question is the album's method, not only its influence. It is the same
operation as isolating a byproduct — take the part of an existing work that was
never meant to carry weight and let it be the whole thing. Track 3 is the
clearest case: twenty seconds of singing, held open until it is a field.

### Learning to let things linger

Every genre carries its own calculation of how long a thing may go on and how
soon something has to happen. A pop song has one, a classical piece has another,
and I know both well enough to run them without thinking. Ambient and drone have
a third, and I do not have it yet.

The symptom is that I cut things short because I assume the listener will get
bored — while I do not get bored listening to ambient. So the instinct is
miscalibrated, not the form, and the correction is to give more space than feels
right. "Mouth out the other" is where I let this happen: I stopped trimming it
and let it run past the length the rack had been arranged to. This is the main
craft thing I am still learning as someone now making drone rather than only
listening to it.

## Reuse, byproducts, and the life of a practice

*Desiderata* grew partly from techniques and byproducts produced while making
*After Green*, a clean-core album that still has one song left to finish.

Making a larger work creates rejected experiments, barely audible layers, and
other byproducts. Isolating one of those layers can reveal a piece of its own.
Recorded material does not have to be waste: it can be cut up, manipulated,
and reused.

The same is true in visual practice. A sketch does not have to become the final
work to remain useful; it can be scanned, transformed, cut apart, and used
again. After practicing for long enough, a personal archive of material begins
to exist on hand. The accumulated work of the practice becomes material for
future practice.

## Source as concept

The title poem, the sound idea, and the visual idea were written separately. As
the album got made I let them answer each other's questions, and the answers
came out of the sources.

The working method is this: a track needs a vocal bed, which raises the question
of where the vocals come from and what kind of vocals get processed. Even when
the destination is dronal mush — and mush is the goal — it matters conceptually
where the material came from. The vocals I choose, what is said in them, why
they were chosen, and how they move all matter, and they keep mattering after
they stop being intelligible.

Worked through the album:

- The vocal organ can span the whole spectrum, which is what a track about
  everything wants. *anything about this* is the only track that uses it.
- "Theia" is about a difficult birth, which is what an album asking why one is
  here wants at its end.
- "Perihelion" is about closest approach before separation, which is what a
  track about passing through without being broken down wants.
- kíkik is already voice-and-cicada, so using it puts the album's question into
  the material rather than on top of it.

This is close to sympathetic magic, and close to conceptual art — except that in
conceptual art the process and source are nearly the whole work, and here I am
also going for a specific texture and will not sacrifice it. When the two
conflict, texture wins and then the concept is asked to catch up. That happened
once, in track 5, and it is written up there.

## The bathroom recording

One source recording connects *Desiderata* and *After Green*. It was a short
song—about twenty seconds—recorded in the tiled bathroom of my family’s old
house, from which we were later evicted. The room contributed its own tiled
echoes and the singing was raw rather than highly polished.

That recording was the seed of *After Green*. Its melodic intervals became the
pitch set used by that album.

For *Desiderata*, I slowed the vocal with PaulStretch and added more reverb,
turning the brief recording into an evolving soundscape two or three minutes
long. I layered copies and shifted one down several octaves. At that scale,
what had been melody became a field of long tones. The overlapping versions
filled different parts of the spectrum.

The same source is treated differently in the two projects. *After Green*
emphasizes its calming and reassuring qualities. *Desiderata* focuses on what
is strange or unsettling within it.

Some of this section may ultimately belong in separate notes for *After Green*.

## Track notes

### 1. I could begin wanting

This piece is made entirely from recordings of my breath. Breath is the moment
before voice: the possibility that something vocal, or a wanting, could begin.

I used Meta Chamber and Horn of Plenty. One allows reverb and pre-verb or a
back-and-forth movement around the source; the other is a stationarizer. The
processed breathing begins to resemble a shore or the washing of waves.
Pre-verb lets the sound swell into the breath and reverb lets it continue out
of the breath, extending it beyond the shape of a natural human inhalation or
exhalation. It remains recognizably my breath but behaves strangely.

The piece also reuses reversed breaths from my 2018 score for *Palibot Libot*.
That film follows money rather than a single person: its perspective changes
each time money changes hands. In the score, a reversed breath marked each
exchange. Those breaths are now peppered through “I could begin wanting,”
alongside the newer, wave-like processed breaths.

### 2. anything about this

The bed is the vocal organ *xyhnthesizer*: my own voice sampled at each key and
played as an instrument. Twelve voices hold all twelve tones at once, spread from bass to
treble so the chord covers the spectrum instead of clustering. The open voicing
comes from wanting a Whitacre-sized choral chord, but what happens inside it is
a landscape rather than choral writing. This is the only track on the album that
uses the organ.

The chord begins in 12-TET and leaves it — a parody of equal temperament in the
sense that it keeps the form of a tuning system while ceasing to obey one. Each
voice drifts off 12-TET on its own clock: first into Pythagorean, then into a
constructed Japanese and Filipino hybrid, then into an inharmonic lattice where
the stack reads as a spectrum rather than a chord. The voices reach each stage
at different moments, so there is no point at which the tuning changes, only a
later point at which it has changed. The deviations stay small, so the drift is
a condition rather than an event.

The score is written in Tabota as a free-time chronological document and
exported as twelve MIDI channels, each with its own pitch bend, which is how the
organ can be played microtonally without retuning the instrument itself. The
generator is at `F:\xyh\ephemeralrenders\anything-about-this\` (see
`anything-about-this.md` there); it also documents the four tunings and marks
which parts of the hybrid are invented rather than sourced.

The finished piece is that bed with reverb, running about six minutes.

I made it last, after tracks 1, 3, 4, and 5 were finished, and then laid
manipulated pieces of each of those tracks over the bed as added texture. So the
track carries fragments of every other track on the album. That is why this is
the one called "anything about this": *this* is the universe, whatever a person
is born into, and the track that names it holds everything else on the record,
and by extension everyone else in it.

The borrowing is also spectral repair. A sustained sampled vowel looped like a
Mellotron has a low ceiling: past roughly 700–1000 Hz there is little content in
it, and the ways of manufacturing some — distortion, extreme high-shelf EQ — are
audible as processing. So the highs were borrowed rather than synthesised. Cut
and pitched-up fragments of the other tracks, track 1 most of all because it is
all breath, supply the hiss and air above the organ. The conceptual reason for
quoting the album inside this track and the practical reason for needing those
frequencies are the same operation.

Whether the twelve-voice spread reads as cosmic is unresolved. That was the
intent; I am not sure it lands.

### 3. world wet stone

This is the stretched and layered bathroom recording.

The title has a material relationship to the source: a voice sung into the
hard, reflective tiles of a shower, with wet stone as both surface and sonic
condition. The recording also holds the absent house and the fact of my
family’s eviction.

The piece has a second life in *After Green*, but here its unsettling qualities
come forward.

### 4. undigested through one

#### The voice

The fermented voice is extracted from **"Perihelion,"** an old song of mine from
2018–19, since re-released under the XYH name and on Spotify with its lyrics. I
pulled the vocal out of that track; that vocal is what goes through the
Gurgulator and is then vocoded against the carrier below.

"Perihelion" is already cosmic and already existential. Perihelion is the point
of closest approach before the orbit carries the body away again, and the song
uses it for a kind of love that is uneasy and about loss: you are at the nearest
point you will ever be to a person, before you had not met them and after you
will drift apart. Existence as transit, and not wanting to be here.

That is why it was the right vocal to take. The words being sung, and the reason
they were sung, stay in the material even when the material is rotted past
intelligibility.

#### The tool

The render tool for this track is at
`F:\xyh\ephemeralrenders\undigested\` (see `undigested.md` there). Voice is fermented — a destructive pass that rewrites the
source buffer into itself, so the material irreversibly ages — then granulated
into a field and given space. The rot can run *during* the render, so the end of
a take is a different material than its start.

Bubbles are a separate layer, synthesised from bubble radius rather than
noise-shaped: a bubble's pitch rises as it collapses, and radius sets frequency,
so one control runs continuously from carbonation to stomach. Bubbles can be put
into the take directly, or written into the substrate first so they ferment and
get granulated along with the voice.

The rot mechanism is forked from Gurgulator in the Sgueltch suite. Light
fermentation is the point here rather than the extreme — the track is
*undigested*: material passing through a gut without being broken down.

The source material was rebuilt rather than retuned. The old vocoder bed
was a major chord, and a major chord cannot be moved into microtonal intervals
after the fact without separating its notes out of a mix — which is what
Melodyne's polyphonic mode attempts, and which vocoded material is a poor
candidate for. Since the carrier was authored here, the tuning still exists
upstream.

The order of operations followed from that. The bed was written first in Tabota,
then run through the Gurgulator rot — authored, then digested. Trying it the
other way, taking finished vocoded material and gurgulating it, was what failed:
the effects change the timbre and leave the interval alone.

The new carrier is four-part close harmony in just intonation, generated by
`F:\xyh\ephemeralrenders\close-harmony\`. Close voicing rather than the
spectrum-wide spread of *anything about this*, because tuning is only audible
when partials are close enough to beat. Barbershop is the reference and is
already a microtonal practice: its ring is coincident partials reinforcing
instead of beating, and its signature chord is a dominant seventh tuned 4:5:6:7,
whose seventh sits 31 cents under the tempered one.

The passages move between just intonation and 12-TET — locked and ringing at one
end, beating and separate at the other. That is the slow event: not a chord
progression but whether the chord coheres. It also answers why the old vocoded
material was rejected for being too musical, since detuning a triad by tens of
cents destroys its triadic identity while leaving the timbre intact.

The voice can also be made the boiling material itself, rather than having
bubbles laid over it. A glide stream forked from *Roil* bends each grain's
playback rate — either coherently, so the whole field tilts as one mass, or
decorrelated, so it roils internally without going anywhere. Grains can also
take a bubble's own trajectory, rising in pitch as they decay, which makes the
voice become the bubble instead of accompanying it.

In the finished piece the bass is doubled. The close voicing put the material
high, and the track needed body under it. Bubbles ended up in two registers, a
low set and a higher set, with popping bubbles arriving late — just before the
denouement. They are pure effect: the Gurgulator rot in the `undigested` tool,
not the bubble-radius synthesis the tool also offers and not a recording of
anything bubbling. That was the surprise of the track — the processing alone
makes bubbles.

Spectral density is the compositional parameter here, in place of harmonic
motion. There are stretches where the spectrum deliberately thins, and the move
out of them is to bring the bass back as body and put whooshes on top. Whether
the spectrum is full or thin at a given moment is the thing being composed, and
either state is fine as long as it is chosen. This is a piece with something
close to a climactic shape; I have been trying to avoid those, and it is harder
to avoid than it looks.

#### Technical record

Kept because it is the part that will not be reconstructable from listening.

The carrier chord as it actually sounds, at the default fundamental of 65.41 Hz
(C2), is `C4 +0¢ · E4 −14¢ · G4 +2¢ · A♯4 −31¢` — four voices spanning 969
cents, closer together than an octave. In strict just intonation all six pairs
of voices beat at **exactly** zero: that silence is the ring. Driven to 12-TET
the major-third pair beats at 10.4 Hz and the top pair at 53 Hz, which is why
the septimal intervals are the ones that suffer most under equal temperament.

Six passages were generated, in `ephemeralrenders/close-harmony/out/`:

| | |
|---|---|
| A-hold-lock | static, just intonation ↔ 12-TET and back |
| B-hold-toward | static, one way into 12-TET and stays |
| C-hold-tight | static, the same chord an octave up (8:10:12:14) |
| D-drop-fifth | moving, up a just fifth and back |
| E-descending-7ths | moving, the barbershop circle of just fifths |
| F-septimal | moving, 7:9:11:13 — no 12-TET spelling exists for it |

**All six are in the finished track.** Each variation was rendered separately
and the results crossfaded, so a passage becomes the personality of a section
rather than a setting chosen once for the whole piece. This is what stands in
for form here: the sections differ by how the chord is tuned and whether it
coheres, not by what the chord is. It is also why the six exist at all — they
were made as alternatives and used as a sequence.

**The bend range is the thing that breaks silently.** Each voice needs its own
synth instance, because a MIDI channel carries one pitch-bend value and these
four bend independently. The passages that restate their chords fit inside a ±2
semitone bend range; a passage that *glides* a whole just fifth needs ±8, and
the range has to be set on the receiving instrument as well — Kontakt ignores
the RPN the exporter writes. If a rebuilt version ever sounds flattened or
stuck, this is the first thing to check.

Modulator source was `PerihelionE_stems_Vocals.wav` (243.5s) with thirteen raw
takes from the same sessions alongside it. Worth knowing for any future
vocoding: **the raw takes are the better modulator.** A stem carries reverb, and
reverb smears the formants a vocoder reads, so consonants turn to mush.

The same music exists three times over — as generator parameters, as a Tabota
score, and as MIDI inside the Reaper project — and they are not peers. The
generator is the source of truth, Tabota is the interchange, the MIDI and the
`.rpp` are build outputs. Editing MIDI in the project forks it from the score
with no way back; regenerate instead.

#### The clicks, and what reverb did with them

The released track contains renders from both before and after a click fix in
the `undigested` tool. Two bugs were found on 2026-07-31 — every synthesised
bubble began with a step discontinuity of 0.23 out of silence, and the
bubble-grain envelope stopped at 0.094 instead of zero and was then cut dead —
and by then the pre-fix material was already placed. Reverb made the clicking
inaudible, so it stayed; the later additions are post-fix.

Worth being exact about what reverb did, because it did not remove anything. A
click is a broadband impulse, and a long tail does not delete it — it spreads
its energy across time. What was a foreground transient becomes an *excitation*
of the space. In a drone with a large reverb that is not damage control; it is
how the tail gets fed. The bug was, in effect, an impulse generator, and the
piece already wanted one.

Two consequences. The energy is still in the file, so a different master, a much
louder system, or later dynamics processing could bring it back toward audibility
— worth knowing rather than worth acting on. And if that kind of excitation is
ever wanted deliberately, it should be made deliberately, not by leaving an
envelope unterminated.

### 5. mouth out the other

The mouth is especially important to an ambient album made only from vocal
material.

The title is enjambed off track 4 — *undigested through one / mouth out the
other* — so the piece is a passage rather than a treatment. A vocal take is put
through a chain of transformations, and position along the chain is position in
time. Every stage is a mouth: a cavity something is spoken through, which
changes what comes out without being the thing that speaks.

The slow event is a direction reversal, not a progression. For the first third
the voice is the modulator and a material is the carrier, so the material learns
to speak. For the last third the material is the modulator and the voice is the
carrier, so the voice keeps its energy and loses its formants. The exchange
happens once. That is the same class of event as the just intonation / 12-TET
coherence swing in *undigested*: the question is whether identity holds, not
where the harmony goes.

The chain is animistic, or techno-animistic: the voice enters one mouth and
comes out of another that belongs to something else, and then another. What it
passes through is a procession of kinds of body — flesh, stone, wet stone,
ocean, wind, metal, electromagnetic hum. Body, then mineral, then water and air,
then machine. Each holds about a minute and a half and crossfades over forty
seconds into the next. Wet stone is deliberate: track 3 is "world wet stone,"
and the surface the voice was sung into there becomes the thing it is spoken
through here.

#### The spine

The spine is the vocal transformation from **"Theia,"** a track on *Eosforos*, my
EP on the Hadean eon — the period in which a protoplanet strikes the Earth and
the debris becomes the Moon. "Theia" is partly a lullaby and partly a
vocalisation of difficulty, specifically the difficulty of childbirth, since the
impact is the eon's birth event.

I chose it because *Desiderata* is bodily and cosmic at the same time, and its
existential question is a waking-up-to-the-world question. A track that is
already both a lullaby and a labour is the right thing to end the album on.

The source is hybrid before anything here touches it. In "Theia" my voice morphs
between itself and **Roil**, my own single-oscillator synth, and back. So the
first mouth in the procession was already in place: a voice that becomes an
oscillator.

A layered alternative exists — the whole recording session stacked and
detuned against itself by under a quarter tone, thickening into one voice rather
than building a chord — and is kept in reserve in case a single take reads too
thin.

One long chain turned out to be the wrong shape. Every second of a serial chain
is the same processing, and no amount of care in any one stage fixes that; it
just makes the monotony well made. So the chain is severed and the voice feeds
three branches at once — the vocoder with its materials, a ring modulator, and a
chorus — each ending in its own space. The ring modulator's frequency climbs
across the piece from a pulse of a couple of hertz up into audio rate, which is
where it stops being a tremolo and starts throwing frequencies that belong to
neither the voice nor the carrier. The chorus opens from two mouths to seven.
Those are slow, and they are the only things moving.

A second vocal take arrives dry near the end. The other mouth. Untransformed,
after everything else has already gone through.

The processing rack lives at `F:\xyh\ephemeralrenders\mouth-out-the-other\` (see
`mouth-out-the-other.md` there). Unlike the tools for tracks 2 and 4 it is not a
renderer but a pre-wired Reaper project — the vocoder, the stretcher, and the
resonance reverb already exist as plugins, so only the two things that did not
exist were written: `mouth_fm`, audio-rate FM whose modulator is arbitrary audio
rather than an LFO, and `mouth_mouths`, a chorus written as a count of mouths
whose voices never realign. Source takes come from *Eosforos* — `theia.rpp` —
which is the album's own byproduct principle applied across projects.

The transient carriers are stationarized through Horn of Plenty before they are
used. An impulsive carrier makes a vocoder spit, and worse, it imposes rhythm on
a drone. The routing in the finished piece is my own rather than the rack's
generated version; the rack supplied the parts.

The best single sound in the piece is the voice vocoded through screeching
metal.

#### Two other speakers

Two of my other tools enter the track, both re-rendered for it rather than
dropped in as finished pieces, and both already crossings rather than raw
sources.

**kíkik** (`F:\xyh\kikik\`) maps my speech onto cicada attacks: the insect's
stridulation is read as a score and the grains dropped on its hits are cut from
voice. What comes out is hard to sort into cicada or voice — I speak through the
cicada. Its own poem is about leaving and returning to find the time changed,
which is the right thing to have in the last track. It is my speaking voice
there, not singing.

**glossolalia** (the *rabble* variant, `glossolalia-rabble.html`) supplies speech
with no speaker: vowels lock at birth and then random-walk, and breath-driven
voices flock into clusters that take conversational turns. I made a rabble of
letters, recorded it off the running page, and put the recording through
**Metachamber** for space and effects. That layer arrives around the
four-to-five minute mark.

#### Why the obvious version was wrong

Following the concept strictly, this track would have used "Theia" and nothing
else, and the kíkik layer would have been "Theia" re-rendered through the
cicadas. I did not do that, for a reason that was purely about texture: the same
singing sample gives the same timbre wherever it is put, and by that point in
the track I had had enough of it. I needed a different timbral quality, so I
reached for a different source.

What I reached for happens to be voice-and-cicada, which is the album's own
subject. The textural problem asked the question and the concept answered it,
which is the reverse of the direction I usually work in. It is also why the
singing-voice version of kíkik stays unmade: putting "Theia" into it would
undo the one thing that layer is there to do. If I make it, it belongs to a
separate piece.

So the track holds three ways of speaking — a voice hybridised with an
oscillator, speech crossed with an insect, and synthetic speech that never had a
body — alongside the vocoder's materials. All of them are interpretations of the
same question: what counts as speaking, and what things can speak.

#### Consequences

This is the album's techno-animist track, and the only one whose sound sources
are not exclusively my body. Everywhere else the material is voice and breath
and nothing else. Here the voice meets materials and materials speak through the
voice.

The materials are still never heard as themselves. Nothing here is a field
recording playing: it is an ocean with a voice through it, metal with a voice
through it. The carriers are only audible in the form the voice gives them.

It is also the least stable. The other four hold as constant beds; this one has
events in it — things that sound like dropping, like metal — which is what
happens when inorganic sources are sampled into a vocal album. The kíkik layer
in particular is percussive while staying textural. By the last track the album
is no longer in drone territory.

Ending here is a choice about the album's argument. *Desiderata* is otherwise
close to antinatalist — a body asking what it is doing here — and it ends with
the voice transferring out of itself into oceans, cicadas, metal. That felt
correct.

## Album art

The cover is a collage of my own photographs, cropped down and then run through
GaHueMa.

The makeup photographs are from the pandemic, when I was trying lip gloss
colours. The base under everything is my favourite NYX Macaron pastel lippie,
*Black Sesame*, a light grey with a yellow-green cast and an unusual amount of
white in it for a grey. Other colours were layered over it. What reaches the
cover is a blue lip, modulated from that grey base, and a desaturated but glossy
pink. A green lip was made and did not make it. The gloss is the point — the
hyper-glossy look is what I was after. The mouth is open, which an album made
only from vocal material has to have.

The rest is photographs of my body, warped together into a field. Individual
folds are legible — a clavicle, a shoulder — but the body is not. The lighting
varies across the source photographs, and my olive skin reads almost grey in
several of them. That is the effect I wanted: desaturated, slightly alien flesh.

The finished collage was too busy, with eyes and other identifiable elements in
it, so the cover is a small crop of it. At that scale it is an abstract field
that is close-up and distant at once, which is the same condition as the sound.

The full collage is kept. It could be a back cover, or a page, or go into the
album as an image — Bandcamp allows editing after release, so this can be
decided later. By the album's own byproduct principle the discarded 90% is
material, not waste.

The crop then went through **GaHueMa**, my hue attractor/repulsor, with the
multiplier raised, in OKLCH rather than HSL. OKLCH gives smoother gradations
between hues; HSL is the choice when you want the harder look. That pass
produced the iridescent passages. I took the result back into Photoshop and
masked it, so iridescence sits where I wanted it and the rest returns to the
unprocessed flesh.

This closes the loop with the album's initial image: desaturated flesh becoming
iridescent under light.

## Material tried and rejected

I had made a vocoded version of Phoebe Bridgers’s “Smoke Signals,” and the first
plan for *undigested through one* was to gurgulate it. It did not work, and the
reason is specific rather than a matter of taste: the bed underneath is a major
chord, and no amount of effect processing separates a chord back into its
intervals. Whatever is done to the timbre, the interval is still a major or
minor third. The replacement is the pair described under track 4: a carrier
written in Tabota, and "Perihelion" as the voice. The whole track had to be
rewritten in Tabota so the tuning existed
upstream, and only then processed.

I also tried easing cut-up pieces of it into the sequence generally. Same
outcome: it brought melody and harmonic progression into focus and loosened the
drone concept.

It stayed out of the album and may be useful for a future work.

## Porosity, not purity

The album is made of voice, but it is not a statement about the purity of the
human. It is the opposite one. The mouth and the body here are cybernetic and
cyborg in the sense of being **porous** — open at the edges, taking in and
letting through — which is what most of my work is about.

That is what the vocoders and the effects are for, and it is why nothing on the
record is heard as itself. A voice that only ever appears fused with an
oscillator, an insect, a synthetic speaker, a sheet of metal, is a voice with no
sealed boundary. The processing is not decoration on a vocal album; it is the
claim.

**The tension the album runs on:** the poem is about refusal, and about a mutual
organ rejection between body and world — and the body it describes is
continuously porous with that world. It cannot keep the world out even while
refusing it. Those two things do not resolve, and the album does not resolve
them. *Desiderata* names a desiring thing, and desire is exactly what is open at
the edge.

### The self becomes a world

The album's subject is the fraught condition of being brought into a world like
this one — a self arriving in a world. But the form does the opposite thing to
it. Turning the voice into drone ambient makes a space out of the voice, so the
self stops being a thing in a world and becomes a world.

The mechanism is ordinary and is the reason ambient works at all. Held long
enough, with few details to latch onto, a sound stops being an object and starts
being an environment. It becomes wallpaper in the useful sense. Applied to a
voice, that means the voice ceases to be someone speaking in a place and becomes
the place.

So the relation inverts across the album: it is about the self in the world, and
it is made by turning a self into a world. That inversion is the strongest
statement of the porosity, because a boundary that can be crossed in both
directions was never a boundary. This is also why the record has to stay near
drone. A piece with melody or progression keeps the voice legible as a figure,
and a figure cannot become a ground.

This also answers what the ending is doing. The voice transferring out of itself
into oceans and cicadas and metal is neither consolation nor a further refusal.
It is the porosity, stated last.

## Poetics of the title and track list

The word *desiderata* may have first arrived through the erotic quality of the
fleshscape. From there, the work began to ask about the difficulty of existing
in a body—particularly my own experience of that difficulty—and about a kind
of mutual organ rejection between body and world.

The five-line poem feels cosmic and existential. It may concern rejection, or
the rejection of a want:

- What does a body desire in this world?
- Is there something to desire?
- Is desire wired into the body, or is it chosen?
- If it is chosen, why choose to desire?

These are deliberately open themes rather than questions the album needs to
answer. The compactness of the poem allows them to remain active without being
closed.

The material nouns—world, wet, stone, mouth—keep the poem in contact with
physical matter. Against them, “I could begin wanting” holds desire as a
possibility rather than a certainty.
