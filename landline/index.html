    Most of this is the line itself, and it is meant to sit at the edge of hearing rather than below it. Headphones, in a quiet room, are the condition it was built for. The volume control is a plain output gain: at 0 dB you are hearing the levels the piece was made at, and nothing else is in the path. Raising it far enough pushes the loudest fragments into a clipping guard, which the readout reports as it happens — past that point the amplification is changing the piece and not only its loudness.

    The piece is built from the ITU-T Recommendations that govern how two modems open a connection. Nothing in it is sampled. The V.8 answer tone, the V.34 line probe and the V.21 capability exchange are synthesised from their published definitions.

    The V.34 probe carries no data. It is a signal a machine emits to find out what the channel between it and another machine is. The receiver measures amplitude and noise across the band and from that measurement chooses the rate it is able to speak.

    A telephone line degrades across the performance. A modem attempts a connection every twenty to thirty seconds; each attempt probes the line, measures what returns, and negotiates whatever rate the measurement allows. The descent from 24,000 to 300 bit/s is not composed. It is what the measurement produces, which is why the connection recovers when the weather eases, and why past a threshold nothing can be negotiated at all. The log above is that measurement, shown as the playhead reaches it.

    No attempt is heard whole. What is placed are fragments cut out of it, none longer than a second, scattered across a window wider than the attempt itself, so that attempts overlap and their edges cannot be found by ear. The process is complete; the audition of it is partial.

    The scale comes from lowercase, and specifically from Steve Roden’s Forms of Paper (2001), made for a branch library out of the sound of book pages being handled. Roden described that material by way of Rilke’s “inconsiderable things” — what would ordinarily not be attended to at all.

    Here the position is the telephone’s rather than a listener’s. The line is what the instrument has instead of hearing, and the piece is closer to what it would make of a night of failed connections than to a recording of one. We are listening in on that.

    The score, and performances of it

    The score is the procedure rather than a transcription: it states what has to happen without naming a language, an instrument or a medium, marks the numbers the Recommendations fix, and leaves every other quantity as an open range. So there is no recording that is the work. Each visit here draws a seed, a duration between five and eleven minutes, and a storm, and plays the performance that follows from them. The seed is printed above; the link beside it reproduces that same performance.
