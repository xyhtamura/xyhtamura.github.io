    Most of this is the line itself, calibrated to sit near the edge of hearing. The piece is designed for headphones in a quiet room. The volume control is an unweighted output gain: 0 dB outputs the piece as rendered, without additional processing in the signal path. Raising the gain pushes the loudest fragments into a clipping guard, which the readout reports as it acts. Above −3 dBFS, the guard applies hyperbolic tangent shaping to peak amplitudes.

    The piece is synthesised from the ITU-T Recommendations that govern how two modems open a connection. The V.8 answer tone, the V.34 line probe, and the V.21 capability exchange are generated directly from their published specifications without pre-recorded samples.

    The V.34 probe carries no data. It evaluates the physical transmission channel between two modems before data exchange. The receiver measures amplitude and noise across twenty-one discrete spectral bins, then negotiates the highest sustainable data rate.

    A telephone subscriber loop degrades across the performance. A modem initiates a connection attempt every twenty to thirty seconds; each attempt probes the line, measures what returns, and negotiates whatever rate the measurement allows. The descent from 24,000 to 300 bit/s follows directly from channel measurements: when simulated weather eases, the negotiated rate recovers; when degradation exceeds the receiver's threshold, negotiation fails entirely. The on-screen log displays each attempt as the playhead reaches it.

    Each attempt is synthesised and evaluated in full, but sounded only as brief fragments. Fragments under one second are scattered across a temporal window wider than the attempt interval, overlapping successive attempts so their boundaries cannot be distinguished by ear.

    The dynamic scale follows lowercase sound practice, in particular Steve Roden’s Forms of Paper (2001), which constructed a quiet acoustic work from recordings of handled book pages. Roden drew on Rainer Maria Rilke’s phrase “inconsiderable things” for material that ordinarily goes unnoticed.

    The perspective is that of the telephone itself. The electrical behavior of the line constitutes the instrument’s sensory register, framing a sequence of failing connections across a storm.
