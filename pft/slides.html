Xyh Tamura

Artist-researcher · creative technologist · composer

Xyh Tamura is a Filipino/Ilonggo-Japanese artist-researcher and composer based in Manila. The practice investigates memory, relational personhood, and the presence of absence across electronic literature, sound, and digital traces. In dialogue with TeleAbsence, the work engages loss and separation through surviving traces without reconstructing synthetic likenesses of the absent. Filipino relational concepts of loób (shared interiority) and kapwa (shared identity) frame virtuality not as a substitute for the physical, but as a theory of what survives a body.

Electronic literature · intermedia suite

Of Another Shore 2025→

The present as a shore between inaccessible pasts and futures · memory, deep time, and ancestral adjacency

Of Another Shore positions the present as a porous shore between inaccessible pasts and futures. The dead, the dreamed, the ancestral, and the not-yet-arrived are kept adjacent within a single scrolling panorama structured across five vertical strata—from sky through shelf to geological sediment.

The suite draws on Filipino-Japanese family inheritance, loss, and deep time. Rather than reconstructing deceased individuals, each interactive node (such as Dream Window Return and body clocks) holds memory as an addressable territory, activating relations from the living visitor's end through traces, seasonal rhythms, and non-simulated encounters.

Electronic literature · disaster afterlives

Shook 2024–2025

Afterlives of disaster · 1990 Luzon, 2011 Tōhoku, and 2020 Taal events · inherited trauma through interfaces

Shook examines the afterlives of geophysical disaster across the Pacific Rim, using the 1990 Luzon earthquake, the 2011 Tōhoku disaster, and the 2020 Taal eruption as temporal lighthouses. It maps how catastrophic rupture survives across generations as bodily habitus, ambient dread, and mediated recollection.

The browser interface arranges raw archival news reels, seismographic traces, and redacted poetry in movable windows. Blackout editing and interactive timing force the reader to reckon with absence and trauma without sensationalized visual reconstruction, turning standard browser viewports into commemorative instruments.

Algorithmic poetry · divination as interface

Cutline 2025–2026

Live generative oracle deck assembled from public network fragments · divination as interface

Cutline treats divination as an interface. The visitor draws an oracle assembled from live public network fragments and interprets the resulting juxtapositions as an omen.

The work does not reconstruct an absent individual, synthesize an artificial persona, or claim that an entity speaks through the machine. It operates as an adjacent precursor to TeleAbsence's illusory communication: encounters with loss and memory are mediated through public linguistic traces and the reader's active interpretation, rather than synthetic simulations of the dead.

Research · philosophy of technology

From Interiority to Interaction 2024–2025

Published in Philosophy & Digitality, 2025 · Denkwerkstatt Berlin

The article examines how personhood is attributed to synthetic entities through social, ritual, and linguistic interaction rather than through resemblance to an autonomous human interior.

Analysing cases including Sony’s AIBO robot funerals in Japanese temples, the android Buddhist priest Mindar at Kōdai-ji, and Geminoid telepresence systems, the paper demonstrates that agency and presence emerge as relational effects stabilized by human practices, institutional framing, and communicative affordances. This theoretical spine anchors the claim that technological presence does not require humanoid mimicry.

Research · affect and social philosophy

Feeling Together 2025

Presented at Nanyang Technological University, 2025

Feeling Together investigates relational models of emotion, selfhood, and intersubjectivity in Philippine social philosophy, focusing on the Tagalog constructs of loób (relational interiority) and kapwa (shared identity / being-with-the-other).

Through qualitative interviews, literary texts, and autoethnography scoped to Metro Manila, the study shows how selfhood is maintained across separation, bereavement, and migration. The concept of loób offers an indigenous framework for TeleAbsence: holding presence across distance not through physical objects alone, but through affective circuits activated between living participants.

Research, place, and installation

Tanim-Kalye 2025

Quezon City Biennial · exhibited at Carinderia Sefali, Krus na Ligas · developed with residents, the local governing unit, and botanical experts from the UP Institute of Biology

Commissioned for the Quezon City Biennial and installed at Carinderia Sefali in Barangay Krus na Ligas, Tanim-Kalye reconstructs the historical street grid using inverted photographic glyphs of culturally and ecologically significant flora. Developed with botanists from the UP Institute of Biology, barangay officials, and neighborhood elders, the project documents species tied to community history and medicinal practice. Transparent acetate maps mounted across the eatery's street-facing window turn daylight into the display system, making absent flora addressable in place.

Fieldwork — herbarium archives, barangay hall, community interviews, and a 1969 household photograph.

Film and live score

Manifest 2024–2025

Short film and videopoem by Bea Mariano and Xyh Tamura · live score by Xyh Tamura · presented at Search Mindscape Foundation and WHYNoT

A collaborative short film and videopoem by Bea Mariano and Xyh Tamura, Manifest montages the underwater wreck of the USS Samuel B. Roberts, high-rise construction across Metro Manila, microbial cultures, and archival military footage. The title activates four simultaneous registers: the cargo manifest, ghostly manifestation, materialization, and American manifest destiny in the Pacific. Performed live with analog synthesizers, voice, and hydrophone samples, the score layers asynchronous drone textures and polyrhythmic pulses that track visual cuts while sustaining autonomous musical development.

Screened to an audience, and at WHYNoT with the printed programme.

Essay on spectral technologies and digital survival
