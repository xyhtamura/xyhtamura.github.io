Cutaway, through Gordon Matta-Clark and The Sims

1.  
What did the carpenters imagine  
would arrive to hold the joists  
in place once the central chimney  
was drop-forged through the subfloor? Do they  
suppose a roof beam balances on habit alone,  
or that twelve tons of unreasonably placed  
masonry hangs suspended by a slender wire  
of flax while the walls are dragged outward  
square by square, intersecting someone  
finishing a cigarette  
listening to frooby noop on the curb?  

2.  
At cleavage, a dwelling divides into two  
unequal hulls, The first  
to drop: forty years of flowered oilcloth.  
The second, the plumb line’s faith  
in gravity. Then: an deletion  
three inches wide, sawn  
clean through the clapboard,  
through the lath, admitting  
a noon glare straight into the fruit cellar  
to strike the coal heap, soon to  
ignite the property  
value and nevertheless remains usable.  

3.  
(a) The foundation indemnifies the tenant against sudden sky.  
(b) The sky is a structural defect in the ceiling.  
(c) A room without four continuous walls is not recognized as a room.  

4.  
Half the porch tilts four degrees south  
clipping into jacks. Between the severed  
halves, a carnal draft  
rifles the medicine chest: iodine, dry sponges,  
a rusted razor, the patent leaflet for a rupture  
brace, stuck to damp wallpaper.  
Zero salvage to show for eighty years of shelter  
except this geometric wedge of daylight  
splitting the kitchen linoleum down  
through the unpainted world to raw bedrock.
