# Longitude suite

Working notes for a possible suite of computational poems about prime
meridians, standardized time, territorial division, and the date line. The
suite began inside the Taper #17 submissions, but it is not limited to Taper or
to the 2 KB format.

## Curatorial rule

One poem should isolate one historical operation. If a fact or mechanism feels
as though it requires another artwork or another statement, remove it from the
current poem and record it here.

Regional groupings are case studies, not claims that an operation occurred only
in that region. Each statement must identify the particular institutions,
places, and period that its mechanism can support. The suite does not need to
cover the world evenly.

The recurring subject can remain longitude, but the active element should
change from poem to poem:

| Work | Regional field | Active element | Operation | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Stealing Prime* | Colonial Asia | Origin | Zero moves and changes within local systems | Taper #17, built |
| *Drawing the Day* | Pacific | Boundary | A date seam attempts to constitute itself | Taper #17, prototype |
| *370 Leagues* (working title) | Atlantic / South America | Partition line | A territorial claim cannot locate itself | Parked |
| Africa partition work | Specific African partition cases | Grid or boundary | Lines allocate territory from incomplete information | Research needed |
| Americas work or works | North and South American institutions | Competing centres | Meridians synchronize, persist, or surrender authority | Research needed |

## Stealing Prime

The poem studies five cases in which a European reference point became
unstable within Asian colonial systems. Bombay resisted standardized time;
Phù Liễn relocated a legal reference through an observatory; Manila crossed a
change of date; Goa adopted the neighbouring Indian system; and Batavia became
a colonial prime meridian.

The places remain fixed while five zeros random-walk from European origins.
This reversal is the poem's operation: the destination does not receive a
stable standard radiating from Europe. The origin moves, arrives, and is counted
there.

Do not add territorial partitions, general histories of world time, or examples
whose main operation is a fixed boundary. Those belong elsewhere in the suite.

## Drawing the Day

The International Date Line has no single international legal authority.
States choose the dates and UTC offsets they observe; the line on a world map
summarizes the resulting boundary. See [NOAA's overview](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/international-date-line.html)
and the [IANA Time Zone Database](https://www.iana.org/time-zones).

The poem treats that summary as a problem repeatedly attempted rather than a
line already given. A path starts at the North Pole on 180°, makes seventeen
southward decisions under a restoring pull toward the antimeridian, and tries
to pass through schematic island fields. The points are permeable constraints:
the path can enter the gaps between them, but it stops if a segment touches a
point. A path that avoids every point reaches the South Pole. Both outcomes
remain as traces; no counter summarizes them.

“Success” is only the poem's formal result. It does not mean that a generated
line is politically or historically correct. The constraints expose why no
geometric line alone can settle a civil date.

The prime meridian remains absent but structurally determines the poem. The
antimeridian is its antipode; change the adopted zero and the attempted seam
moves with it.

Questions for the prototype:

- Should island groups be named or remain schematic constraints?
- Should historical changes alter the constraint field over time?
- Should a successful line persist more strongly than a failed one, or should
  the failures dominate the accumulated image?
- Does a binary success/failure result clarify the procedure or imply an
  authority that the poem is meant to question?

## Tordesillas / *370 Leagues*

Removed from *Stealing Prime* on 2026-08-05 because territorial partition is a
different operation from a moving origin. The removed form was a fixed,
translucent band centred near 48°08′W and labelled `TORDESILLAS 1494`. Its width
represented published estimates produced by uncertainty about the league and
the Cape Verde starting island.

The strongest retained idea is a partition that asserts territorial certainty
while remaining unable to locate itself. A future work could accumulate
incompatible placements of the treaty line, allow claims to change when the
measurement changes, or make the line divide places before it can determine
which places lie on either side.

Research before drafting must distinguish the treaty text, later cartographic
interpretations, the Zaragoza antimeridian, and retrospective explanations of
Brazil's boundary. Do not reduce the work to “this line is why Brazil speaks
Portuguese.”

## Africa partition work

The parked subject is the use of latitude, longitude, rivers, and other mapped
features in particular colonial boundary agreements. The mechanism should draw,
allocate, or revise territory. It should not reuse the moving-zero mechanic.

The earlier sentence “Africa was not given a clock; it was given the grid” was
removed because it makes a continent-wide contrast that the evidence cannot
support. A future poem needs specific agreements, actors, maps, and dates. The
Berlin Conference may supply context, but it should not be described as the
meeting that directly drew every later African border.

Possible operations:

- a boundary propagates from coordinates that cannot be established on the
  ground;
- several European descriptions generate incompatible lines across one place;
- a geometric border repeatedly meets settlements, routes, watersheds, or prior
  political territories that its source document does not contain;
- the map resolves cleanly while the represented territory does not.

## Americas work or works

North and South America may require separate poems. Do not combine them solely
to complete a continental sequence.

A North American work might address competing national meridians, the
Washington meridian, railway standard time, or Alaska's change of date after
the 1867 transfer. Its likely operation is synchronization among competing
centres rather than a zero travelling from metropole to colony.

A South American work needs research into national observatories, national
prime meridians, nautical practice, and the adoption of international time
standards. Tordesillas belongs here only if the work remains about partition;
it should not be used as a general explanation of later South American
longitude.

The useful suite distinction is formal:

- *Stealing Prime*: the origin moves.
- *Drawing the Day*: the seam is generated.
- Tordesillas: the partition cannot locate itself.
- Africa: the grid allocates territory.
- Americas: competing centres negotiate or surrender reference authority.

These distinctions are provisional. A researched case should be dropped or
moved when its historical operation does not match the poem that contains it.
