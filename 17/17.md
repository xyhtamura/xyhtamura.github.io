## Piece 1 notes — prime song

- Voice source: additive partials at prime harmonics 1·2·3·5·7·11 of f0 (aliquoto move).
- Formants: two bandpass filters; F1 = 55·{5,7,11,13}, F2 = 55·{13,17,19,23,29,37,43} Hz.
  Vowel glyph derived from the prime pair via articulatory grid (F1→height, F2→backness).
- Consonants: filtered noise bursts at 470·{2,3,5,7,11,13} Hz, glyphs `hkpʃts`.
- Scale: 17 random pitches, 98–450 Hz (2^2.2 span), sorted; melodic walk over indices
  with per-line arch/trough bias (antemelos contour), phrase-final lengthening,
  breath rests between lines.
- Verse structure: 3|5|7 lines × 3|5|7 syllables.
- One persistent WebAudio graph; song schedules automation, never rebuilds nodes.
- Byte check: `powershell -File check-bytes.ps1 prime-song/index.html`

## Piece 2 notes — Stealing Prime

Titled "Stealing the Prime" until 2026-08-04; folder was `stealing-the-prime/`.

- Five European prime meridians paired with Asian places where longitude or
  standardized time became consequential: Greenwich→Bombay (resistance),
  Paris→Phù Liễn (legislation), San Fernando→Manila (change of date), Lisbon→Goa
  (adoption from a neighbouring colonial system), and Amsterdam→Batavia (a new
  colonial origin). The pairings deliberately superimpose different periods and
  institutions rather than depicting a single imperial network at one moment.
- No map, no graticule, no caption. Ten city dots with names, and five
  full-height `0°` meridian lines. x uses the equirectangular conversion
  `x = (lon+180)/3.6`, with positions rounded to compact map coordinates; y is
  loosely fitted and carries no claim.
- Each zero random-walks from its source to its sink. Per step the zero moves
  `dir*1 + uniform(-2.5, 2.5)` map units — constant drift, no gravity.
- The drift was a progress-proportional ramp (`0.4 + 1.6*progress`, so 4× stronger
  at the sink than at the source) until 2026-08-04. It measurably worked: mean
  advance per step ran 0.48 → 1.98 across the journey, and the zeros took 1161
  steps inside the first fifth of the route against 215 in the last. It was
  simplified out at Xyh's direction — the image did not need to be gravitational,
  only directed — which freed 23 B and removed a latent runaway. Under the ramp,
  a zero that strayed more than 1.25 arc-lengths behind its source would have had
  negative drift and accelerated away permanently, so the ramp needed a clamp at
  0.4 to stay safe; measured strays reached 1.62 arc-lengths, well past that
  threshold. Constant drift cannot reverse and needs no clamp.
- Measured after the change: advance is flat at ~1.0 through every fifth of the
  route, 30% of steps still go backwards, strays behind the source still reach
  1.27 arc-lengths, and first arrivals fall between 38 and 163 s. Still visibly a
  walk, just an evenly paced one.
- Position is carried unwrapped (`X`) and wrapped only for rendering, so a walk
  can cross the Pacific seam without the arrival test breaking. San Fernando's
  direction is set west, which routes it off the left edge and back in at the
  right — the Manila galleon, and the map's cut left visible.
- On arrival the zero **holds on the sink city for one of its own steps**
  (1.7–3.1 s) before returning to the source, so the theft has a visible moment
  rather than only a counter increment.
- The sink label carries the count: `BOMBAY 13`. This is the accumulation and
  the leaderboard both.
- A second line under each sink prints the live time offset from its wandering
  zero: `+4:48`. Longitude is time — 15° is one hour — so the on-screen gap
  between a zero and its colony is already a clock reading; the label only names
  it. Offsets are signed and wrapped to ±12 h, so a zero that wanders far enough
  west flips its colony's sign, which is the date-line condition the Philippines
  actually resolved by deleting 31 December 1844.
- Departure offsets against the historical values: Bombay +4:48 (Bombay Time was
  +4:51), Phù Liễn +6:58 from Paris (the 1906 legal meridian was +6:57:09),
  Goa +5:31 from Lisbon (+5:32), Manila +8:38 from San Fernando (+8:29), and
  Batavia +6:58 from Amsterdam (+6:48). The differences come from the compact,
  rounded x positions.
- Waypoint cities were cut on 2026-08-04 as clutter, after being built twice
  (routed, then inert). Ten city dots remain: five sources, five sinks.
- The Tordesillas band was removed on 2026-08-05. It introduced territorial
  partition into a poem whose operation is a moving origin, so it required a
  second statement and weakened the regional thesis. The implementation and
  research are retained in [longitude-suite.md](longitude-suite.md) as the basis
  of a separate partition poem.
- Emergent and worth keeping: at the moment of arrival the sink offset reads
  `+0:00`, because the origin is standing on the colony. The theft completes
  exactly when the colony's time equals the metropole's.
- Ship `i` steps when `tick % p_i == 0` on a 100 ms tick, `p ∈ {17,19,23,29,31}`.
  All five step together only every 6,678,671 ticks — 7.73 days.
- Motion between steps is interpolated. Each ship keeps `a` (position before the
  step) and `b` (after); the rendered position is `a + (b-a)·(u % p_i / p_i)`
  where `u = t/100` unfloored, so the tween phase needs no stored state — it
  falls out of the same modulus that schedules the step. Measured per-frame
  movement at 60 fps: mean 0.008–0.022 map units. The only remaining
  discontinuities are the two intended ones, the seam wrap and the snap home
  from sink to source, and the snap-home jumps measure exactly the arc lengths
  (20/29/23/29), which confirms nothing else teleports.
- The prime schedule survives the smoothing untouched: interpolation changes how
  position is drawn between decisions, not when decisions happen. Tick length is
  the one dial — realignment in days is `tick_ms × 6,678,671 / 86,400,000`. At
  100 ms that is 7.73 days; halving the tick doubles the rate at which the walk
  changes heading and halves the figure.
- Measured over a simulated 10 minutes: first arrivals at 25.5, 98.8, 140.3,
  58.0, 83.7 s; counts 13/8/4/8/5. Manila is always slowest (arc 64 vs 20–29).
  Different every run.
- Sizing: `width:min(100vw,(100vh - 52px)*1.42)` with `height:auto` +
  `aspect-ratio:10/7`. The 52px subtracts the fixed template header; a plain
  `130vh` overflowed at 800×450. Verified fitting at 800×450, 1280×720, and
  980-layout mobile.
- With the caption gone there is no fixed-px text left, so everything scales
  with the viewBox and the piece stays legible on a phone despite the Taper
  template having no viewport meta.
- Byte check: `powershell -File check-bytes.ps1 stealing-prime/index.html`

## Piece 4 notes — Same Time Tomorrow

- Opens with the historical 47-kana iroha in legible 7/5/6/5/7/5/7/5 lines.
- Japanese title: `いろは時計` (“Iroha Clock”). An English translation fades to
  zero over the first 47-tick revolution.
- Clock state at tick `t`: offset `t mod 47`; stride `1 + floor(t/47) mod 46`.
- Every state visits all 47 character identities exactly once. All 2,162 affine
  readings occur before the original offset and stride coincide again.
- At returns 1 and 2, the alphabet modernizes persistently: `ゐ→い`, then `ゑ→え`.
- At later return `r`, prime `P_(r-2)` addresses the one-based position
  `P_(r-2) mod 47` (remainder zero means position 47). A random current kana is
  grafted over that sign.
- Each sign retains two grafts in FIFO order, so base plus overlays show at most
  three letter histories. A third graft sheds the oldest overlay.
- At tick 2,162 the original order returns carrying two spelling changes and 44
  grafting events. The clock is periodic; the prime-residue address sequence is
  not, and the grafted kana are contingent.
- Primes are generated incrementally by trial division: each candidate is tested
  for divisors only through its square root, and the search resumes after the
  previously found prime rather than beginning again at 2.
- This gives the clock a latent computational aging. Chronological ticks remain
  regular while linguistic changes accumulate irreversibly, but discovering each
  future mutation generally demands more searching as the primes grow. The cost
  is negligible during an ordinary viewing; in the poem's distant future, time's
  increasing computational burden is genuinely produced by its mathematics
  rather than represented by an artificial slowdown.
- Byte check: `powershell -File check-bytes.ps1 same-time-tomorrow/index.html`

## Piece 5 notes — Drawing the Day

- Title: “Drawing the Day.” Working slug: `antimeridian/`; final filename:
  `drawing_the_day.html`.
- The International Date Line is treated as the result of separate civil-date
  choices rather than a single internationally legislated line.
- Each attempt begins at `x=50` (180°) and makes 17 decisions from north to south.
  At every step, `uniform(-7,7)` moves the path while `(50-x)/7` pulls it toward
  the antimeridian.
- Four schematic island fields act as point constraints near decisions 3, 7, 11,
  and 15. Their x and y positions vary within each field. Collision is tested
  against each generated line segment, so a path can pass between dots but stops
  if it comes within 1.2 map units of one.
- The coordinates are deliberately schematic. Aleutians, Kiribati, Samoa, and
  Chathams name reasons for bends in mapped date lines; the image does not claim
  to plot islands or legal borders.
- Both outcomes persist without a numerical summary. The poem retains the 47 most
  recent attempts; pole-to-pole paths are darker and thicker, while stopped paths
  accumulate as shorter traces around the point that ended them.
- A 100,000-attempt simulation with the current points produced 19.1% successes.
  The distribution is tuned for visible successes without making passage routine.
- The prime meridian is absent but structurally necessary: the antimeridian and
  restoring pull exist only as its antipode. Seventeen decisions also bind the
  procedure to the issue number.
- Verified in motion at 1280×720: after 20 seconds, 4 paths reached the bottom,
  11 stopped at points, and one was still drawing. The counter was absent, the
  page did not overflow, and the console remained clean. Verified again at
  375×667 with the complete composition visible and no page overflow.
- Byte check: 1528/2048 B with `check-bytes.ps1` (520 B free).
- Still open: decide whether the four place names are too explanatory and whether
  the red/green distinction grades the outcomes more strongly than the work needs.
  W3C validation remains for the pre-submission pass.
